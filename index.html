Congress, House and Senate

Congress is the whole lawmaking body. The House and Senate are its two chambers. A bill must pass both in identical form, then the president signs or vetoes it. Congress can override a veto with a two-thirds vote.

What Democrats need

House: 218 seats for a majority, about a net gain of 3 to 4 seats.

Senate: a net gain of 4 seats, to reach 51. Democrats must hold all 13 of their own seats up this year and flip 4 Republican-held ones.

On the ballot: all 435 House seats, 35 Senate seats (22 Republican-held, 13 Democratic-held), and 36 governorships.

Other things to remember

Midterm vs. presidential election

A midterm falls halfway through a president's term, with no presidential race. Turnout is lower (about 41% vs. 58% on average since 1972), and the president's party usually loses seats.

How the president is chosen

Voters choose electors state by state. There are 538 electors and a candidate needs 270. In 48 states and DC the statewide winner takes all electors. Maine and Nebraska split theirs by district. A candidate can win the presidency and lose the national popular vote, as in 2000 and 2016.

How governors are chosen

By direct statewide popular vote, with no Electoral College. Texas governors serve four years with no term limit. Greg Abbott (R) runs for a fourth term against Gina Hinojosa (D). The governor is a state job, not part of Congress.

Redistricting

Redistricting means redrawing House district lines. It normally happens once a decade, but this cycle several states redrew mid-decade. Texas redrew to make 5 more seats lean Republican (designed to move its 38 seats from 25R-13D to 30R-8D if voting matches 2024). California redrew in response to favor Democrats. The number of seats per state does not change until the next census. Voters still decide each seat.

India comparison

Lok Sabha (543, directly elected, up to 5 years) is like the House. Rajya Sabha (245, chosen by state legislators plus 12 nominated, 6 years) is like the Senate. India's Prime Minister is the leader of the Lok Sabha majority. The US president is elected separately and serves a fixed four years.
